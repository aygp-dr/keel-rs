#+title: SICP 1.1 against the keel kernel
#+property: header-args:keel :results value

This is the Emacs answer to the WAForth VS Code extension's =.wafnb=
notebook: Org Babel, which Emacs already has. Evaluate a block with
=C-c C-c=.

Unlike that extension's notebook controllers, which are constructed
stateless and give every cell a fresh kernel, the kernel here is one
session shared by every block, by =M-x keel-repl= and by every
=keel-eval-= command. A Forth stack you cannot carry between blocks is
not much of a Forth stack. Use =:reset yes= on a block that wants to
start clean.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp :results silent
  (add-to-list 'load-path (expand-file-name ".." default-directory))
  (require 'ob-keel)
  (org-babel-do-load-languages 'org-babel-load-languages
                               '((keel . t) (emacs-lisp . t)))
#+end_src

* 1.1.1 Expressions

Scheme's prefix combination is Forth's postfix. =(+ 137 349)=:

#+begin_src keel :reset yes
  137 349 +
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
| 486 |

=(+ 21 35 12 7)=. Scheme's variadic =+= becomes a fold; the stack is
the argument list.

#+begin_src keel :reset yes
  21 35 + 12 + 7 +
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
| 75 |

* 1.1.1 Nesting

=(+ (* 2 4) (+ 3 5))=. There is no =*=; =um*= leaves a double-cell
product =( lo hi )=, and for a product that fits in one cell the high
cell is zero, so =um* += adds that zero back and leaves the low cell.

#+begin_src keel :reset yes
  2 4 um* + 3 5 + +
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
| 16 |

* Subtraction, derived

=(- 1000 334)=. No =-=, no =negate=, no =dup=. =sp@ @= duplicates the
top of stack, =nand= with itself inverts it, =1 += completes the two's
complement negation, and =+= subtracts.

#+begin_src keel :reset yes
  1000 334 sp@ @ nand 1 + +
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
| 666 |

* Division

=um/mod= is =( ud-lo ud-hi u -- u-rem u-quot )=, so push the numerator,
then =0= for its high cell, then the divisor. =(/ (+ 5 (* 2 4)) (+ 3 (-
6 2)))= is 13/7: remainder 6, quotient 1.

#+begin_src keel :reset yes
  5 2 4 um* + + 0 3 6 2 sp@ @ nand 1 + + + um/mod
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
| 6 | 1 |

* Output

=emit= appends a byte to the kernel's output. Ask for =:results output=
to collect it instead of the stack.

#+begin_src keel :reset yes :results output
  72 101 108 108 111 emit emit emit emit emit
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
: olleH

Note the order: =emit= takes the top of stack, so five pushes followed
by five =emit=s print backwards. Getting "Hello" out needs the bytes
emitted as they are pushed:

#+begin_src keel :reset yes :results output
  72 emit 101 emit 108 emit 108 emit 111 emit
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
: Hello

* 1.1.4 Compound procedures

=(define (square x) (* x x))= cannot be written as =: square sp@ @ um* + ;=:
a colon definition needs the outer interpreter, which is a frames word
and arrives at milestone M4. Try it and the evaluator says so:

#+begin_src keel :reset yes
  : square sp@ @ um* + ;
#+end_src

What *does* work is the kernel-level equivalent --- compile the thread
by hand, then reach it through =execute=. The xts are =sp@ @ um* +=,
which is =2 0 11 5=; =exit= is appended for you.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (keel-reset)
  (setq sicp-square (keel-compile-thread (list 2 0 11 5)))
  (keel-push 5)
  (keel-push sicp-square)
  (alist-get 'stack (keel-eval-string "execute"))
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
| 25 |

Called again on a different argument, through the same body address:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (keel-reset)
  (let ((square (keel-compile-thread (list 2 0 11 5))))
    (mapcar (lambda (n)
              (keel-push n)
              (keel-push square)
              (keel-eval-string "execute")
              (keel-pop))
            '(1 2 3 4 5 10 21)))
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
| 1 | 4 | 9 | 16 | 25 | 100 | 441 |

Note that =keel-reset= is called once, *before* compiling, and never
inside the loop: =reset= rewinds HERE to the start of the dictionary,
which invalidates every body address compiled before it. Resetting
between calls would leave =square= pointing at cells the next
compilation overwrites. That is the kernel's own =reset= semantics, not
a choice made here.

So a compound procedure exists --- a body address, reached through
=execute=. What is missing is the *name* and the parser that would bind
it. That is the whole of milestone M4.

* Where SICP 1.1 stops

| SICP                            | needs                                                  |
|---------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| =(define (square x) ...)=       | the outer interpreter: a name and a colon compiler     |
| =(define (abs x) (cond ...))=   | branching; the frames layer builds IF from =execute=   |
| =(define (factorial n) ...)=    | recursion, so a named definition that can call itself  |
| 1.1.7 =sqrt= by Newton's method | IF, recursion, and a signed =/=                        |

None of that is missing from this Emacs package. It is missing from the
kernel, on purpose, until the frames layer lands.
